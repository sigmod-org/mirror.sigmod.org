    Transportation:

    Santa Barbara Airport is located about 10 miles west of downtown Santa Barbara. There are a few ways to get to the hotel from the Airport:

    • A free shuttle service provided by the hotel (you should make a reservation for the shuttle pickup at the airport at the time of making your hotel reservation).

    • A typical cab ride from the airport costs about $28-33 and takes about 15-20 minutes.

    • Super Ride (phone: 805-683-9636) costs about $19.

    • To drive, exit the airport and turn right, following the sign "to Santa Barbara". Take Freeway 217 (if you take the wrong direction you will enter UCSB, make a U-turn after the parking kiosk, and you will be on the right direction). 217 will merge into Freeway 101. Drive for about 9 miles, and exit at Milpas. Turn right at Milpas, pass the railway cross, turn right at the next traffic light (Calle Puerto Valarta) and the hotel parking lot is on the right.

    Los Angeles International Airport is about 100 miles away. There are a few possibilities of going to the conference hotel:

    • Santa Barbara Airbus between LAX and Santa Barbara Airport (with a stop at the Radisson Hotel, 1111, E. Cabrillo Blvd, 2-3 blocks from the conference hotel) about every two hours. The round trip fare is $64 (prepaid) or $69 (non-prepaid). If you plan to take the bus, you are however advised to make a reservation since sometimes the bus is full.

    • To drive, exit LAX and take Freeway 405 north, change to 101 north, exit at Milpas. Turn left (roundabout) at Milpas, pass the railway cross, turn right at the next traffic light (Calle Puerto Valarta) and the hotel parking lot is on the right.

    About Santa Barbara

    Santa Barbara is a "get out in the sunshine and fresh air" place, with all the activities that implies. The average annual rainfall is about 15 inches, and the average rainfall for May is only 0.2 inches.

    Santa Barbara has a unique geographic situation. For roughly 40 miles on either side of Santa Barbara, the Pacific coastline runs almost due East/West. This alignment moderates the ocean currents that flow past Santa Barbara's front door. Towering mountains, incredibly close to the sea, shelter the coastal strip from inland heat and cold. The result is a comfortable year-round average temperature of 64 degrees, with an average maximum of 74 and an average minimum of 56 degrees. The highs and lows in May are around 70 and 50 degrees, respectively. For May 21, the record high is 78 degrees (1989) and the record low is 42 degrees (1942).

    Points of interest:

    • Santa Barbara County Courthouse. Timeless Spanish-Moorish masterpieces with colorful corridors, historic mural room, 85 ft. observation tower. Great views from the clock tower. 1100 Anacapa St.
    • Zoological Gardens. Over 700 animals on the grounds of a former beachside estate. 500 Ninos Dr.
    • El Presidio De Santa Barbara Historic Park. The Presidio includes buildings that were part of the last Spanish mili-tary outpost in the New World. The chapel contains re-stored 18th century decorations. 123 E. Canon Perdido St.
    • ARLINGTON CENTER FOR THE PERFORMING ARTS. Originally built in 1875 the interior contains a mock Spanish village. Curved ceiling is "star studded" and creates an outdoor illusion. 1317 State St.
    • Santa Barbara County is a world-class touring and wine tasting destination, renowned for distinguished wines and golden countryside dotted with quaint western towns and friendly locals. Today's wine boom centers in the Santa Ynez and Santa Maria Valleys, boasting 56 wineries. Acclaimed for its Chardonnay and Pinot Noir varietals, the area also produces Merlot, Cabernet Franc, and Rhone varietals such as Syrah.
    • Santa Barbara Historical Museum. Charming adobe com-plex, built in 1817, houses one of the finest collections of regional history in California. 136 E. De La Guerra St.
    • STEARNS WHARF & SEA CENTER. Oldest operating wharf on the West coast. Sea Center offers a glimpse into marine life that inhabits the Santa Barbara Channel. Nature Conservancy includes displays dedicated to the preservation of rare and endangered species and habitat types. At the foot of State St.
    • SANTA BARBARA MUSEUM OF NATURAL HISTORY. Nationally renowned museum specializing in North American West Coast and California natural history. Special theme exhibits. 2559 Puesta del Sol Rd.
